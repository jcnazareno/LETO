# D01–D04 candidate values

**Supporting calculation: 28 October 2026.** Values below apply to the stated 50,000 km reference conditions. They are study candidates, not the current proposal's recording-quality requirements or calibrated sensor performance. The [workbook](d01_d04_research.xlsx) contains 39 evidence records and the 192-event published flash catalogue.

**Shared reference conditions:** a provisional **600–900 nm** top-hat band, **3000 K** spectrum, instantaneous onset followed by an exponential **100 ms decay constant**, and band-integrated background radiance **≤10⁻³ W/m²/sr**. This profile is a calculation fixture, not a universal lunar-flash model. Validate an initial **2000–4500 K**, approximately **1–1000 ms** envelope, with nonexponential profiles and backgrounds outside the reference case. Real response curves and thermal operating limits remain to be established.

Use received exposure as the primary radiometric quantity. **E₂π = 2πr²H_B** is a line-of-sight hemispheric-equivalent energy; interpreting it as total emitted energy assumes angular emission. It is neither kinetic energy nor mass. Those require separate luminous-efficiency, spectrum, velocity and emission-geometry assumptions.

## What the evidence supports

The 2024 NELIOTA analysis reports practical R≈11.2 and I≈10.2 magnitude limits under variable observing conditions, with 23 ms exposures and 10 ms readout. These are not portable detection-probability specifications. The [Zadko study, now verified as PASA 43 e098](https://doi.org/10.1017/pasa.2026.10211), resolves four bright flashes at 200–250 fps (§§3–5, Table 2; author v1 PDF pp4–9). Its calibration limitations prevent treating cadence as a joint faint-flash photometry benchmark. **No matched-condition D01 superiority is established here.**

The [2026 terrain study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19635v3) fits different mare/highland cooling times and discusses up to ~30% energy differences at equal peaks (PDF p3, Fig. 3). This motivates calibrated tails and controlled terrain samples; it does not prove a terrain-dependent luminous-efficiency law.

LUMIO already includes dual-channel confirmation and temperature estimation. Use its 2023 radiometric model as a labelled design comparator and the [2024 payload update](https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/f609816c-3cf5-4468-93bc-12ee09e38135/PANCM01-24.pdf) for the revised 450–800 / 850–950 nm channels (Tables 1–3). [ESA’s March 2026 update](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/Shaping_the_Future/A_leap_forward_for_Lumio_Phase_C_officially_launched) places engineering-model verification in Phase C. Final measured payload curves were not established by this review.

## Attainability and next decisions

An **illustrative 200 mm aperture, 500 mm focal length**, 30% effective photon-detection fraction and nine-pixel footprint yield ideal detection **SNR≈21** at 10³ J, and **SNR≈14** in the onset-aligned 1 ms peak bin at 10⁴ J. The field is only **0.71°×0.40°** (about 620 km × 350 km at 50,000 km, ≈0.6% of the lunar surface at any instant); this calculation does not establish global exposure. At fixed optics/background, the same received-exposure target corresponds to **40 / 1000 / 2560 J** at **10,000 / 50,000 / 80,000 km**.

The localization example allocates 0.2 pixel centroid error, 0.5 arcsec attitude knowledge, 0.3 arcsec boresight error, 0.2 arcsec residual jitter and 50 m orbit/map/height terms, each at 1σ. Its 95% semimajor is **0.47 km at nadir**, **0.94 km at 60° emission angle**, and **1.37 km at 70°**. These assumed allocations need validation; a pixel scale is insufficient evidence of accuracy.

**The unresolved feasibility issue is calibrated event-sensor response at the actual faint photon flux.** [Pedersen et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18183201) provide a transient-calibration method, not a lunar photometric guarantee (§§4–5). Manufacturer latency conditions and low-bandwidth sensitivity demonstrations cannot certify the proposed joint values. **Current assessment:** with a realistic photopic efficacy for this red band (≈30 lm/W instead of the 683 lm/W upper bound behind the workbook's 4.1 lx figure), the reference geometry places roughly 0.003 lx of background and ≈0.02 lx (10³ J) to ≈0.2 lx (10⁴ J) of peak flash light on a pixel. That is far below the 5 lx condition at which Sony specifies IMX636 behaviour (where it already allows up to 1 ms latency), so the 1 ms / 10⁴ J option is not supported by current sensor evidence at a 50,000 km range. Much closer viewing geometry changes this; see the 30 October science-benefit study. No new sensor experiment or calibrated v2e run was performed.

Decisions recorded in the workbook:

- Supporting systems are permitted **only if necessary**, including ground references. Ground co-observation cannot directly supply a reference for unobserved farside events; an onboard intensity anchor or independently validated event-only method may still be needed.
- Both faint-count and fast-physics priorities, and both D04 outcomes, remain open **by user choice**. A second spectral channel is not automatically selected.
- Acceptance evidence remains open. Independent versus ground-cooperative global coverage also remains unresolved (A2).

Before selection, measure joint detection/characterization efficiency and uncertainty, validate geometric confidence regions, and couple them to D05 area-time and D06 samples. Approximately 100 complete independent events per bin gives 10% Poisson counting uncertainty before efficiency/systematic errors. Lifetime, number of spacecraft and coverage feasibility cannot yet be inferred. Reproduction commands and model limitations are in the [analysis README](../../analysis/d01_d04_research/README.md).

## Extended light-curve and reconstruction findings

The new **Light curves** sheet records five observing cases for four Zadko impacts, including the same E4 flash measured twice. Detected durations are 20–85 ms, with delayed peaks, a reported secondary brightening and saturation in E2/E3. The **Reconstruction** sheet records required inputs, six published method/accuracy benchmarks, 16 analytic examples and eight further-analysis targets. Values from papers, illustrative calculations and proposed tests remain labelled separately.

- **Early and later light are distinct measurements.** The 2026 terrain differences emerge from about 50 ms after peak, with a model-dependent slow cooling component near 0.5 s. A [confirmed 2013 event](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stu083) lasted 8.3 s. A universal 100 ms recording window or single exponential is inadequate (E04, E05, E29, E31).
- **An averaged peak is not the instantaneous peak.** An exponential matching a 1.5-mag drop in 4 ms has τ≈2.895 ms. Its onset-aligned 1 ms mean is 84.6% of the initial peak; ≤0.300 ms limits that example's averaging loss to 5%. This is a brighter-subset stretch test, without a new threshold or demonstrated sensor capability.
- **Absolute event reconstruction needs an anchor and a calibrated response.** Contrast integration applies to total pixel intensity, including background. At 12% ON contrast, a 10:1 rise spans about 20 events/pixel; one lost ON increment biases total intensity low by 10.7% before background subtraction. Threshold, faint-light bandwidth, refractory time, loss and reference errors need joint inference (E12–E14, E27–E28, E32).
- **Published reconstruction accuracy is protocol-specific.** Complementary-filter image errors of 7.76%/9.05% use events simulated from 168 Hz video and 20 Hz frames. Later relative image-error reductions are not absolute flash-energy uncertainty. Astronomical event comparisons at 100–500 ms do not validate 1 ms photometry. No lunar event-camera accuracy bound is newly established.
- **Extend the test envelope.** [Initial spectral excesses](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2020.105131) motivate trial ~2000/6000 K mixtures as well as 2000–4500 K single spectra (E30). Proposed tests cover ≤2% unrecorded energy or an explicit bound, adaptive long tails, a trial 200 ms pretrigger buffer and ≤0.1 ms relative spectral-channel offset if needed. These allocations need budget review; no additional channel is selected.

The analytic examples use [lightcurve_inputs.json](../../analysis/d01_d04_research/lightcurve_inputs.json) and [calculate_lightcurves.py](../../analysis/d01_d04_research/calculate_lightcurves.py). Source plots are reproduced as cited excerpts, not digitized measurements. No raw telescope reduction, event-camera reconstruction experiment or calibrated v2e run was performed.

## Impact-energy distribution and missing populations

The [catalogue and audit](../../data/lunar_impact_energy/README.md) compile **all 192 validated rows in the two reviewed NELIOTA tables**, not every survey or all lunar impacts. Report §§1.5–1.6 and workbook Energy distribution / Energy catalogue contain the full observed distribution, energy-conversion scenarios and spatial/time limitations. New evidence is E33–E39.

| Question | Defensible answer |
| --- | --- |
| Smallest measured? | **4200 J of integrated R+I optical energy**, NELIOTA #111 in the compiled tables. At η=0.0015 this corresponds to **2.8 MJ** kinetic energy from rounded light; the source table quotes 3±1 MJ. This is a verified catalogue minimum, not a demonstrated all-time record or 95%-completeness limit. [E33] |
| Observed distribution? | R+I range **4.2–580 kJ**, median **21 kJ**, middle half **14–52.5 kJ**. Counts in [4,10), [10,30), [30,100), [100,300), [300,1000) kJ are **27,88,53,15,9**. The bins have unequal widths. Published nominal-η kinetic estimates span **3–388 MJ**; all are inferred. [E03,E33] |
| Highest? | Strongest recorded optical flash in the reviewed literature: **(6.5±1.0)×10¹⁰ J** inferred KE on 11 September 2013, assuming η=0.002. A much larger **222 m contemporary crater**, formed in 2024, has estimated KE **6.5×10¹³ J**, using crater scaling at 15 km/s and density 3 g/cm³. The latter has no calibrated flash in this record and only a **41-day** occurrence bracket. Neither sets a physical maximum. [E29,E34,E39] |
| Complete intrinsic distribution? | **Unknown.** The observed catalogue mixes the incoming population with energy/profile/spectrum-dependent recovery, missing area-time, confirmation cuts and bright-end loss. No intrinsic power-law slope or universal lower cutoff is established. |
| Smaller impacts missed? | **Yes.** Lunar impact-ejecta measurements and meteoroid models establish a much smaller-particle population. A model-scale 10⁻⁶ g particle at an assumed 20 km/s has **0.2 J** KE, calculated rather than measured as a lunar flash. LDEX's ~0.3 µm grain threshold describes secondary ejecta, not primary impactor size/energy. [E35,E36] |

At fixed light, η=0.0005–0.005 moves kinetic inference by a factor **10**; it is a coherent scale uncertainty if common to all events, not a statistical spread. Variable η may also alter the inferred distribution's shape. R+I is not the proposed 600–900 nm band, so the 1000 J D01 candidate cannot yet be claimed as a measured improvement over the catalogue minimum.

**Temporal and spatial limits:** NELIOTA's 23 ms exposure/10 ms gap samples an ideal 69.7% live fraction; a 1 ms pulse can fit wholly within the gap for 27.3% of random onset phases, before other losses. Its **283.4 h** useful exposure over 6.5 years and approximate **3.11×10⁶ km²** field do not establish global rates. Reported **0.5°** coordinate error is about **15.2 km** meridionally, without a specified confidence level. The 2013 record flash's reported epoch is ±0.01 s and coordinates ±0.2°; crater detection trades much better mapping detail for poor occurrence-time knowledge. Lunar local time, season, source direction and energy selection must be separated from terrain/hemisphere differences. [E03,E29,E34,E37]

The [2026 Chang'E-6 study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-026-03270-z) supports long-term nearside/farside similarity; it does not settle present-day polar or flash-energy distributions. Treat spatial differences as hypotheses. Source-summary discrepancies are explicitly retained: a 1.164 GJ table maximum versus a 1.05 GJ narrative value across η scenarios (the 2024 text discusses its Fig. 23, which also assigns velocities by meteoroid origin and may cover a different sample; the 2024 Table A.2 maximum is 1.027 GJ, so this may be a convention difference rather than a source error), and 146/46 extracted stream labels versus 145/47 in the published rate normalization. Resolve them before source-specific population fitting.

**New study targets, not requirements:** test the provisional mission-band grid **100–10⁶ J**, including the existing D01–D03 points; map 50/90/95% end-to-end recovery and bright losses. Explore ≤5% relative recovery and ≤5% area-time uncertainties. For a rate ratio, two equally populated independent complete bins need about **200 events each** for 10% counting error, or **≈450 each** for a local 3σ test of a 20% difference (linear approximation; a log-ratio test gives ≈540), before systematics. No D01–D04 candidate or science priority is changed.
